# News Style Guide: SES Student Research Spotlights

**Audience:** prospective students, alumni, colleagues, and the NAU
community. Smart readers, not specialists.

## Structure (400–700 words)

1. **Lede:** the finding and the person, in two sentences, no jargon.
2. **Why it matters:** the stakes or the puzzle, in the student's own framing
   where possible.
3. **The work:** what they actually did (field, lab, data), with one vivid
   concrete detail.
4. **A quote or two** from the interview, verbatim and approved.
5. **The road:** challenge, surprise, or turning point.
6. **What's next** for the research or the student.
7. Close with the paper reference (the page renders it automatically from
   `publication_id`).

## Voice

- Active, concrete, warm; short sentences; no press-release superlatives
  ("groundbreaking", "cutting-edge", "novel").
- The student is the protagonist; faculty co-authors appear as mentors and
  collaborators, not the story's center.
- Explain one technical term at most; cut or paraphrase the rest.
- Numbers: round sensibly, give a comparison ("a lake core spanning 15,800
  years, three times the age of writing").
- No em dashes; use commas, colons, or separate sentences.
- Attribute every claim to the paper or the interview; do not editorialize
  about the science's importance beyond what the sources support.

## Mechanics

- Title: the finding, not the process ("Tracking mercury through a Ghanaian
  lake's past", not "SES student publishes paper").
- `summary` frontmatter: one sentence, plain, no colon-splice headlines.
- Student's full name on first mention, then first name.
- Include program and advisor once: "a PhD candidate in Earth Sciences and
  Environmental Sustainability advised by ...".
