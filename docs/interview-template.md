# Interview Question Bank: Student Paper Spotlight

Pick 4–6, tailor them to the paper, and write them in plain language. Always
open with the introduction (who you are, why you're writing, Nick cc'd) and
close by telling the student they'll approve any quotes before publication.

## Core questions

1. In a sentence or two for a general audience: what did you find, and why
   does it matter?
2. What drew you to this question in the first place?
3. What was the most challenging part of this research, and how did you get
   past it?
4. Was there a moment in the field, the lab, or the data where things
   clicked? What did that feel like?
5. What surprised you most in the results?
6. How did working with your advisor and co-authors shape the paper?

## Optional, as relevant

- What should Arizonans (or people in the study region) take away from this?
- What's next for this line of research, or for you?
- What advice would you give an undergrad thinking about grad school in your
  field?
- Is there a photo from your fieldwork or lab you'd like us to feature (that
  you have rights to)?

## Logistics reminders

- Offer a deadline about a week out; one gentle nudge if no reply.
- Accept written answers or a short call (transcribe and confirm quotes).
- Confirm name spelling, program, degree stage, and advisor.
