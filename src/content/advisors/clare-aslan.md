start in Fall 2027.

## Sustainable rangeland management

Across the American West, rangelands and other rural, working landscapes face a
convergence of economic and ecological pressures. On the Colorado Plateau, plant
communities exhibit the stressors of changes in fire regime, broad-scale tree
die-off events, historical overgrazing, and shrub encroachment. At the same
time, rangelands provide essential habitat for wildlife while sustaining rural
livelihoods. In light of the current multidecadal drought, ongoing and long-term
research into sustainable land management practices is increasingly important.

You will help lead a new phase of data collection and analysis aimed at:

- Quantifying the effects of long-term experimental grazing practices on
  biodiversity at multiple taxonomic levels
- Examining sources and patterns of disturbance resilience on rangelands
- Assessing the effectiveness of restoration practices aimed at bolstering
  ecological function across heterogeneous landscapes

## Biodiversity and resilience in the borderlands

The US Southwest currently exhibits extreme warming and drying. Widespread
invasion of non-native plants transforms fire regimes, and historical
overgrazing has led to widespread riparian erosion and loss of surface waters.
Conservation of the region's remarkable diversity requires evidence-based data
on species requirements and emerging global change, as well as an understanding
of ecosystem resilience in the face of interacting stress and disturbance.

You will contribute to ongoing research examining the response of endangered and
culturally important plant species to drought and fire in southeastern Arizona.
Your research may include:

- Examining the effectiveness of fuels treatments and restoration efforts across
  a range of jurisdictions
- Collecting demographic and survival data on focal plant species of concern,
  including culturally important plants
- Assessing biodiversity at multiple taxonomic levels within areas spanning
  gradients of recent disturbance severity
